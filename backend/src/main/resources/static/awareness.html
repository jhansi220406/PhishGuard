NEW THREAT VECTOR 4 min read

QRishing: The Dark Side of Quick Response Codes

QR code phishing ("Quishing") exploits physical curiosity. Attackers paste counterfeit sticker codes over parking meters, restaurant ordering hubs, and payment terminals. Because humans cannot visually read the 2D matrix, they scan blindly, landing on phishing credential harvesters or malware downloaders.

Key Defense: Never scan unverified public stickers. Check destination hostnames on scanner previews before interacting.
COMMON MISCONCEPTION 3 min read

The HTTPS Fallacy: The Green Padlock is Not a Shield

Many users falsely believe that seeing https:// or a browser padlock means a website is safe. In reality, HTTPS only guarantees that the connection between you and the server is encrypted against Wi-Fi eavesdroppers. Today, over 80% of phishing kits obtain free TLS certificates (e.g. Let's Encrypt). The connection is secure, but the destination is fraudulent!

Key Defense: Verify the actual hostname domain against official bank or portal bookmarks, not just the padlock.
DECEPTION TACTIC 5 min read

Typosquatting & Unicode Homoglyph Impersonation

Attackers register domains with subtle misspellings (e.g., microsof-security.com) or exploit internationalized domain names (IDN) by substituting Latin letters with visually identical Cyrillic or Greek characters (e.g. Latin 'a' vs Cyrillic 'а', rendered as Punycode xn--...). To the naked eye in an address bar, they look identical.

Key Defense: Use password managers that bind credentials strictly to exact origin domains; they never auto-fill on lookalikes.
SOCIAL ENGINEERING 3 min read

Artificial Urgency: The Psychological Trap

Phishing messages invariably simulate extreme crisis or FOMO: "Account Suspended in 24 Hours", "Unauthorized Wire Transfer Detected", or "Immediate HR Benefit Update". Attackers engineer high adrenaline states to impair critical cognitive evaluation, causing victims to bypass scrutiny and click.

Key Defense: Pause immediately whenever a message creates urgency. Contact the organization via official, out-of-band channels.
